## Core Idea

When you send a revised document, include a short bullet-point summary outlining what you added, removed, or materially changed.

## Why This Matters

Reviewers operate under time and cognitive constraints. Without guidance, they must manually search for differences, which increases friction and reduces the likelihood that important changes receive focused attention. Providing a concise change summary lowers review cost, improves clarity, and signals professionalism.

## Example From Context

You could include bullets such as:

1. Added a paragraph on page 2 discussing a new topic.
2. Changed the stance of the conclusion.
3. Updated citations in a specific section.

This enables the reviewer to immediately locate and evaluate the substantive changes rather than scanning the entire document.

## How To Apply

- Include a “Summary of Changes” section in the email body when sending revised documents.
- Use concise bullets.
- Focus on substantive edits (new arguments, removed sections, changed conclusions, updated data).
- Reference page numbers or section names for quick navigation.
- Keep it brief to optimize for reviewer efficiency.
