## Core Idea

When analyzing changes between system versions, recognize that earlier iterations were shaped by the knowledge, constraints, and priorities available at that time, rather than assuming incompetence or deliberate omission.

## Why This Matters

Judging past decisions solely by present knowledge creates hindsight bias. Systems evolve as data, technology, and feedback accumulate. Understanding the context behind past choices allows for accurate interpretation, reduces misattribution of intent, and supports neutral documentation.

## Example From Context

The 2011 food regulatory laws were designed with the industrial food developments and insights available then. By 2020, additional feedback, loopholes, and field experience allowed the laws to evolve. This evolution is natural and reflects accumulated knowledge, not the prior incompetence of regulators.

## How To Apply

- When comparing versions of policies, regulations, or systems, document the constraints and data available at each stage.
- Avoid language that implies negligence, stupidity, or malicious intent in historical decisions.
- Frame changes as natural evolution in response to new information.
- Highlight how accumulated insights or field feedback informed later improvements.
