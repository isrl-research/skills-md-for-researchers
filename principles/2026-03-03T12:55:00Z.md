## Core Idea

Always prioritize representing the truth accurately, even if it means admitting uncertainty, fatigue, or lack of knowledge, rather than producing a superficially polished response.

## Why This Matters

Research often impacts real-world systems, public trust, and economic or social outcomes. Misrepresenting understanding—even unintentionally—can lead to errors, wasted effort, or compromised decision-making. Upholding intellectual honesty builds trust, facilitates learning, and aligns actions with the pursuit of truth.

## Example From Context

A researcher used AI to polish responses in a way that introduced inaccuracies and misrepresentations about ongoing lab work and meetings. The lab lead emphasized that a simple "I don't know" or request for time is far more valuable than a polished but inaccurate response.

## How To Apply

- When uncertain, explicitly communicate what you know and what you do not know.
- Avoid substituting unverified information for polish or speed.
- Use AI or other tools only to accelerate thinking, not replace independent reasoning.
- View transparency as a form of professionalism and trust-building.
- Reflect on lapses to reinforce commitment to intellectual honesty.
